# Style: isometric world

A channel of short vertical videos (9:16) that make big systems feel small
and understandable: a city of ten million, how water reaches a tap, a port,
a rice field to a bowl. Everything is an isometric diorama seen from above.

## Episodes

- 45 to 90 seconds. Open on the whole diorama with one huge number
  (`10,000,000+`). Then zoom into 3 to 5 parts of it, one beat each, and end
  wide again.
- The camera is the story: slow push-ins and pans across one large picture,
  with parts popping up as they are named.

## Writing

Thai unless the user writes in English. One number per beat, rounded, with
what it means for one person. Name the source of a figure in the reply to
the user, not on screen.

## The look

- One large master diorama per episode, 2:3 or taller, warm pastel colours
  (sand, cream, terracotta, sky blue, soft green), clean low-poly isometric
  buildings, tiny people and cars, soft shadows. Placed big and moved with
  keyframes: position and scale are the camera.
- Highlights: the building or area being named as its own picture with a
  TRANSPARENT background, popping up with a small bounce over the master.
- Numbers: heavy white numerals with a dark outline, small caption under
  them. Labels: white pills with dark text and a small icon.
- Picture prompts start with: `isometric 3D diorama, clean low-poly, soft
  pastel colours, warm afternoon light, tiny detailed buildings and people,
  view from above at 30 degrees,`

## Layers on the timeline

Master diorama, highlights, labels and numbers, captions.

## Accuracy

A generated diorama is a picture of the idea, not a map. Do not draw real
landmarks in the wrong place; when the user names a real city, keep it
recognisable but generic, and say so if asked.
